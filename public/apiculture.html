Apiculture

A comprehensive guide to beekeeping under varied Indian conditions.

Major Bee Species for Indian Apiculture

Click here to check the full video of Apiculture.

Apis cerana indica

(Indian Hive Bee)
  • Native/Exotic: Native
  • Colony Size: 20,000–30,000 bees
  • Honey Yield: 8–12 kg/colony/season
  • Disease Resistance: Good tolerance to Nosema & Varroa.
  • Management: Suitable for smallholders and low-input hives.

Apis mellifera

(European Honey Bee)
  • Native/Exotic: Exotic
  • Colony Size: 40,000–60,000 bees
  • Honey Yield: 20–30 kg/colony/season
  • Disease Susceptibility: Higher risk of Varroa & Foulbrood.
  • Management: Best in Langstroth hives; needs intensive care.

Other species like Apis dorsata (Giant Bee) and Apis florae (Dwarf Bee) are important native pollinators but are typically not managed in hives for commercial honey production.

Hive Management in Different Climatic Zones

Arid / Semi-arid

Orient hives E-W under shade, provide water trays, and ensure proper ventilation to prevent overheating.

Hot-Humid

Elevate hives on stands for airflow, use screened bottom boards, and control moisture to prevent dampness.

Cool / Temperate

Wrap hives with insulation in winter, orient south-facing for warmth, and use windbreaks.

Common Bee Diseases & Treatments

Disease Signs Treatment & Control
Varroosis Deformed wings, reduced stores Organic acids, drone brood removal
American Foulbrood (AFB) Sunken brood, "ropey" larvae Burn infected frames, antibiotics
European Foulbrood (EFB) Twisted larvae, uneven brood Remove infected combs, antibiotics
Nosemosis Dysentery, reduced foraging Fumagillin, ensure dry hive
